## 0. Short answer

1. **Volume cap: NOT applied; `PROBE_NLEV` stays 15 (top = level 14), `PROBE_DEFLEV` 10.** The ladder routine (`POKE` in `build/hook/ladder_core.py`) is byte-for-byte the generator's, so `PROBE_NLEV 12` would lower the loop's top by the same 4.74 dB (model; 4.6 dB measured on probe402) that the owner asked for on the generator. But the cap's purpose was an ABSOLUTE one ("~4.5 dB too loud ... to avoid distortion or damage", field log line 19390), and at the same ladder level the payload is the quieter source: the loop's default is level 10, the generator's is 6 (both chosen by ear and measurement against the same streamed reference), and the owner has heard the loop's level-14 top and called it "not uncomfortable" (probe312, line 17041) and "reached as before" (probe327, line 17433) while he called the generator's level 14 "4.5 dB too loud". With NLEV 12 the loop would keep ONE step (+1.3 dB) above its default. Section 1 has the table and the chain of evidence; a cap is a one-line change (`PROBE_NLEV`) and a rebuild if the owner's ear wants one (NLEV 14 = top 13 = -1.5 dB is the margin option).
2. **PWRREL as in probe427 is NOT safe in the loop image** (section 2.1, proven by reading and shown in the simulator with a counterfactual build): the loop has a 2.46 s ADPCM chunk (a VM transform into Kalimba port 3) in flight at almost every power-off, and the 16 s audio-busy hold is what keeps the stock's parked prompt (the power-on announcement = a TTS `KalimbaLoad` over our DSP) away from the DSP while our transform still feeds it. probe427's unconditional `RELEASE0` at 0x4002 would let that TTS tear the DSP down under a running chunk (rule 4 of `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/FACTS.md` N033).
3. **The loop image has the same power-on delay bug** (well-supported: the same `RSEND(0x13, 16000)` in STOPT/OFFALL holds the word for 16 s after a power-off; in the stock-world model a stock TTS requested 1 s after the power-off stays parked until 46 s on probe387, section 3.2 row H). The owner reported it on the generator only; he has not reported it on the loop.
4. **probe428 = probe387 + `PWRREL2`** (a new opt-in flag in `build/hook/ladder_ext.py`; probe387 and probe427 rebuild byte-identical with the edited file, proven in section 4): the release goes through a new 15-word routine **RELFREE** that writes `SetAudioBusy(0)` (via `RELEASE0`: only if the word is our task) ONLY when port 3 has no transform (`StreamKalimbaSink(3) = 0` or `TransformFromSink(ksink) = 0`, the handler's own read-only test before every rebuild), and it is called (i) at the power-off 0x4002 (mode ON after the OFF path, mode OFF directly) and (ii) at the free edge of the LAST chunk (the `MESSAGE_STREAM_DISCONNECT` 0x8026 that FREEEDGE already routes to the cave) when the mode is OFF. Result in the model: after a power-off mid-chunk the hold drops when that chunk ends, about 2 s later (section 3.2 row A: `0@32045` for a power-off at 30 000 ms, probe387: 16 s), a stock TTS parked meanwhile is served with `0 chain(s) cut` (row H), and the power-on announcement is therefore delayed by at most the rest of one chunk (about 2.5 s) instead of 16 s. The 16 s REL timer stays as the backstop (a chunk that outlives its free-edge message, the rate-latch corner, `--free-edge audible`).
5. Image: `build/hook/probe428_brown.dfu`, sha256 `327a1bc579cc422459f37293b7316e9dbf723c88f1cadc9fdf2a1f9719fc7013`, 2106 cave words (the count equals probe387's because EXT code is placed over fill words that are counted anyway; 897 words differ: the seeded EXT layout moved), 3947 bytes differ from the official image. ## 1. The volume cap

### 1.1 The ladder is the same code; the numbers

`POKE` (`ladder_core.py` lines 664-691, `PROBE_FINE_ZERO`): level 0 = silent (ratio 0); level j >= 1: tone-mix ratio byte `M[0x28f6]` hi = `30 + 7 * (j % 4)`, tone-copy shift `M[0x29e6]` = `j // 4`. The ratio is a linear multiplier and the shift is a power of two, so gain(j) = 20 log10(ratio) + 6.02 * shift (model; the measured generator ladder of `docs/deep/top-volume-402-2026-10-30.md`, steps 1.2-1.9 dB and top = default(8) + 9.5 dB, agrees with this model's +9.35 dB for 8 -> 14). `PROBE_NLEV` is used in exactly three immediates: `HANDLER` (`cmp AL,#NLEV`, the poll-id test), `CANCELANY` (`cmp AL,#NLEV`, the cancel loop) and `VOL` (`cmp AL,#NLEV-1`, the clamp). Nothing else changes with it.

| level | ratio | shift | gain (model, dB) | vs top 14 | vs the loop default 10 | vs the generator default 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | - | silent | | | |
| 6 | 44 | 1 | 38.89 | -12.04 | -6.02 | 0 (generator default) |
| 10 | 44 | 2 | 44.91 | -6.02 | 0 (loop default) | +6.02 |
| 11 | 51 | 2 | 46.19 | **-4.74** (the NLEV 12 top) | +1.28 | +7.30 |
| 12 | 30 | 3 | 47.60 | -3.33 | +2.69 | +8.71 |
| 13 | 37 | 3 | 49.43 | -1.51 | +4.52 | +10.54 |
| 14 | 44 | 3 | 50.93 | 0 | +6.02 | +12.04 |

So `PROBE_NLEV 12` on the loop = top 11 = -4.74 dB from today's top, exactly the generator's cap in relative terms (proven arithmetic); but the loop's top would then be only +1.28 dB above its default (one press up, ten down), where the generator keeps +7.3 dB (five up).

### 1.2 Is the loop's top too loud in absolute terms? The evidence says no

- Owner on the loop's top: probe312 (24 kHz loop, ladder top shift 4 ratio 21 = 50.5 dB in the same model): "the loudest it gets is not uncomfortable to my ear" (`docs/field-log.md` line 17041, proven). probe327 (the fine ladder of today, DEFLEV 10, NLEV 15, top 14 = 50.9 dB): "4. yes" = "the top level is reached as before" (line 17433, proven). The owner used the loop product (probe327 .. probe367 .. probe387) for days without asking for a cap.
- Owner on the generator's top (probe425, DEFLEV 6, the same level 14): "The highest level is ~4.5db to loud" (line 19390, proven). Same speaker, same ladder cell values, different source: the generator's samples are hotter than the decoded payload's at the same ratio and shift.
- How much hotter: the defaults. The loop's default 10 was accepted by ear (probe318 "perfect", probe327); the generator's default went 10 (probe380: measured +1.1 dB over the loop at the same level 10, `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` section 1) -> 9 -> 8 (probe402, measured -0.46 dB against the streamed GOAL reference, `docs/OVERNIGHT-LOG-2026-10-30.md`) -> 6 (probe424/425: "DEFLEV was chosen by the bass level alone", 424 at 6 = 421 at 8 minus 2.0 dB total, `docs/deep/probe424-hybrid-curve-2026-10-30.md`). Each default was placed at about the GOAL level, so the generator's source gained about four ladder levels (about 6 dB) over the payload between probe380 and probe425 (well-supported; the links are separate measurements with different references, so the uncertainty is a few dB).
- Consequence: the loop's level 14 is at about the generator's level 10, i.e. about 1.3 dB BELOW the top the owner accepted on probe427 (generator level 11), with an uncertainty of about +-2.5 dB. A cap at NLEV 12 would put the loop's top about 6 dB below the accepted loudness and leave one step of range. (well-supported estimate; the owner's ear decides, section 6 step 6.)

### 1.3 Decision and the alternatives

`PROBE_NLEV = 15` unchanged in probe428. If the owner's ear says the loop's top is still too loud: `PROBE_NLEV = 14` (top 13, -1.5 dB, three steps up) or `PROBE_NLEV = 13` (top 12, -3.3 dB); `12` reproduces the generator's relative cap but is the wrong absolute level for this source. Level 15 (NLEV 16, +1.3 dB over today's top) would be a new, never-played rung and is not proposed. Any of these is a one-line change in `build/hook/assemble_probe428.py` and a rebuild; the three immediates are the only words that move in the program (the layout may re-seed).

## 2. PWRREL in the loop

### 2.1 Why probe427's form is unsafe here (proven by reading, shown in the simulator)

What the audio-busy word does in the loop image (unchanged from probe220/312): `GATE` -> `HOLD` sets it to our task 0xBA3C when the first chunk is built; every stock prompt and the stock plugin DISCONNECT are `MessageSendConditionally` on it (N 7.5, Z 10.3 in `docs/deep/poweroff-with-noise-2026-10-30.md` section 2); at an OFF (press, power-off, yield) `STOPT`/`OFFALL` arm `REL` (id 0x13) 16 s later -> `RELEASE0` -> `RELEASE` (`SetAudioBusy(0)` iff the word is ours). The comment at `ladder_core.py` line 70 says why 16 s: "the chunk in flight plays out first". In the loop a chunk is a `TransformAdpcmDecode` from the partition-2 source into `StreamKalimbaSink(3)`; nothing of ours ends it (rule 4: no TransformStop/disconnect of a running transform; probes 173-175), it ends when the source is exhausted: 58,968 bytes x 2 samples / 48 kHz = **2.457 s** (`build/hook/gate_product.py` line 432; 14.742 s if the first chunk after a stock tone runs at the latched 8 kHz, probe177/`--latch late`). The loop rebuilds at every free edge, so at a random power-off a transform holds port 3 with probability close to 1 (model row G: 24 chunks in 60 s, one always in flight).

probe427's `EV_P: bsr RELEASE0` would clear the word at the power-off itself. The stock arm then enters limbo (section 1 of the power-off note) and the next power-on's announcement (`fn_18b0a(0x57)` -> `fn_0df7f`, a TTS: `MessageKalimbaTask(0xB330)` + `KalimbaLoad(NowSpeakTTS)` over the resident DSP) is no longer parked: with a power-on press 3 s after the power-off it runs while our chunk still has up to 2.4 s (or 14 s) to go, i.e. a DSP reload under a running VM transform into port 3. That is the situation rule 4 forbids ("never SPEAK while our transform holds port 3", N033: the prompt is silent at best; what a `KalimbaLoad` does to a running transform's sink is unknown and was never pressed) and the one the 16 s hold was built to avoid. In the generator image there is no transform, which is why the review of probe427 could write "the GEN product has no transform and no chunk; nothing new touches port 3".

Counterfactual in the simulator (probe387 rebuilt with probe427's `PWRREL = True` under a scratch name, `build/night/layout/run_scratch.py probe387 probe9387p EXTRA_FLAGS=PWRREL=True`, never written to `build/hook`): stock-world run H of section 3.2: `t= 31000 STOCK TTS served ... our DSP is replaced (1 chain(s) cut)`, chunk `#12 ... EOF 31000 end 31000 (cut: stock TTS prompt (--tts-at))`. The same run on probe428: `STOCK TTS requested: PARKED on the audio-busy word (0xba3c)` at 31 000, `served ... (0 chain(s) cut)` at 32 045 = the free edge of chunk 12. On probe387: parked until 46 000 (REL).

### 2.2 The design: RELFREE and its two call sites (flag `PWRREL2`, `build/hook/ladder_ext.py`)

Source (three sites; with `PWRREL2` unset the program is probe387's, with `PWRREL` it is probe427's; the two flags are mutually exclusive by assertion):

```
EVTX (enterl -8), the 0x4002 arm:                     EXTH (enterl -8), the 0x8026 arm (FREEEDGE):
  ld AL,@(3,Y) ; cmp AL,#0x4002 ; bne EV_5              cmp AL,#0x8026 ; bne XF_1
  bsr PEEKMODE ; cmp AL,#0 ; beq EV_P                   bsr CANCELANY ; cmp AL,#0 ; beq XF_R      (387: beq XF_X)
  bsr MODETOGX                 (the OFF path, as 387)   add AL,#LEVEL_BASE-1 ; ld AH,#1 ; bsr RSEND ; leavel   (the poll, as 387)
EV_P: bsr RELFREE              (427: bsr RELEASE0)    XF_R: bsr PEEKMODE ; cmp AL,#0 ; bne XF_X   NEW: mode ON -> nothing
  bra EV_X                                                  bsr RELFREE                               NEW
                                                      XF_X: leavel -8

RELFREE (enterl -2, nothing stored):
  ld AL,#3 ; bsr StreamKalimbaSink ; cmp AL,#0 ; beq RF_R        no Kalimba sink = no DSP: nothing of ours in flight
  bsr TransformFromSink ; cmp AL,#0 ; bne RF_X                   a transform holds port 3 (ours, or a stock tone's): keep the hold
RF_R: bsr RELEASE0                                               SetAudioBusy(0) iff the word is 0xBA3C (ladder_core RELEASE)
RF_X: leavel -2
```

Why these two moments and no other: (i) the power-off itself, so that a power-off between chunks or after the DSP is gone releases at once (and the mode-OFF branch covers a power-off inside the 16 s after a press-OFF); (ii) the free edge of the last chunk, because that is the first moment after an OFF at which port 3 is free; the firmware's dispose posts 0x8026 to the task registered on the sink (`MessageSinkTask` in BUILD, probe337; hardware-proven as the poll trigger since then), and `RELFREE` re-reads `TransformFromSink` before writing, so a 0x8026 that arrives before the port is really free changes nothing (REL at 16 s then does the release, as today). `PEEKMODE` at XF_R keeps every mode-ON behaviour identical: while the mode is ON a 0x8026 with no poll pending (STARTWAIT waiting, a restart pending, a stock stream ending on the sink) does nothing, as in probe387 (model row J: an SLC disconnect at 30 s, mode ON, no release at the following free edge; the ENSURE restart re-takes the hold at 34 s as before).

What RELFREE never does: no message is sent (R5/R6 untouched), no DSP write, no `KalimbaPowerOff`, no `TransformStop`/disconnect, no console event; `RELEASE` writes only when the word is ours, so a stock owner (0xBD4E tone, 0xB195 TTS) is never overwritten (row E). The traps it calls (`StreamKalimbaSink`, `TransformFromSink`, `GetAudioBusy`, `SetAudioBusy`) are the ones `ENSURE`, `HANDLER`, `HOLD` and `RELEASE` already call on every chunk.

Cost: 27 program words (RELFREE 15, EV_P 3, the XF_R arm 9). Frames: RELFREE `enterl -2`, no store; the deepest new paths are TRIG(-8) > EVG(-4) > EVTX(-8) > RELFREE(-2) > RELEASE0(-2) > RELEASE(-4) > fn_2f9fb(-4) > fn_2fa15(-2) and HANDLER(-8) > EXTH(-8) > RELFREE(-2) > ... , both shallower than the existing EXTH(-8) > ENSURE(-8) > ENSURE2(-8) > AUTOSTART(-8) > RSEND(-8) > stub path; `python tools/stackdepth.py 0xe46 --frame 8`: `headroom at that point: 316 - 54 = 262 words`, `a cave frame of 8 words: FITS`, unchanged.

### 2.3 What the loop still does NOT do, and why

The announcement is delayed by the rest of the chunk in flight (0 .. 2.46 s; 14.7 s in the 8 kHz latch corner), not zero: the only way to make it zero would be to end the running transform at the power-off, which the standing rules forbid (TransformStop on the TransformFromSink handle Panics 0x4030; disconnecting a running transform is the probe173 runaway). A time-based shortening of `PAYLOAD_RELEASE_MS` (e.g. 3000 ms) was rejected: it is not safe in the latch corner, and the port-check form is exact.

### 3.3 Reading of the orderings (proven from the source)

- Power-off, mode ON: `EVTX` -> `PEEKMODE` (1) -> `MODETOGX` -> `MODETOG` -> `STOPT` (`CANCELANY`, `RSEND(0x13, 16000)`) ; `OFFALL` (cancel 0x20..0x2F, `CANCELANY` until none, re-arm 0x13, `MUTE`: ratio 0 while the chunk plays out) ; `TMREARM fn_12abb` -> `RELFREE` (chunk in flight: nothing) -> `EV_X` -> `EVENTS` (0x4002: nothing) -> back to TRIG -> `PASS` replays `fn_189fb(0x4002)`. Then the stock arm runs as before. At the chunk's EOF the firmware disposes the stream and posts 0x8026 to our task (the sink task set in BUILD) -> `HANDLER` (not a poll id) -> `EXTH` -> `CANCELANY` = 0 -> `XF_R`: `PEEKMODE` = 0 -> `RELFREE`: ksink nonzero, `TransformFromSink` = 0 -> `RELEASE0` -> the word is 0xBA3C -> `SetAudioBusy(0)`. The parked announcement is delivered. REL fires at power-off + 16 s, finds 0 (or a stock owner), does nothing.

## 5. Risks for an independent reviewer

1. **The free edge as a release trigger** is new logic on a message the cave already handles (0x8026 -> a 1 ms poll since probe337). RELFREE re-reads the port before writing, so the only new effect is an earlier `SetAudioBusy(0)` in the mode-OFF state. What then happens earlier (not later than today) is the delivery of parked stock prompts and, in the warm case, the stock plugin DISCONNECT: the same consequences the probe427 review listed for the generator (section 4 there), with the difference that here port 3 has just been read free. Not run on hardware.
2. **The 0x8026 timing**: the message is the firmware's dispose at source EOF; the DSP may still hold a few ms of audio (the "free-before-audible-end" seam, muted by MUTE's ratio 0 after an OFF). A TTS `KalimbaLoad` at that moment is the same thing the stock does at every seam when it unloads our DSP (the NOPLAY/GONE path exists for it, proven on hardware). Hypothesis that it is harmless; it is what happens 16 s later today, and it is what the owner's power-on press provoked on probe427 within 3 s on the generator.
3. **Hypothesis that the loop shows the owner's bug at all**: not reported on the loop; the mechanism is identical (proven) and the model shows it (row H control), so the fix is expected to matter; if the owner never sees the bug on the loop, PWRREL2 costs nothing (row G: the mode-ON loop is unchanged).
4. **The re-layout**: 897 words moved (seeds 9/1/3); every gate above stands on the new image; the same kind of re-layout went to the speaker at every probe since 405.
5. **No cap**: if the owner's ear finds the loop's top too loud after all, the fix is `PROBE_NLEV` (section 1.3); nothing in the image prevents the owner from simply not pressing up.
6. Rollback: probe387 (`c0e3efc81794151c0f8eb2023682e73a35263cbe879abde23d4e19bca235d851`, the loop on unit 2 2026-10-29), or the generator probe427 (`d3de3e67030c599f0ffa780bed0944c2b0521a422ee19a1421cd04adcc5919fc`, needs no partition). Recovery order: `docs/FLASH-PROTOCOL.md`.
